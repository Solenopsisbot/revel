## Constraints

- **Mascot is Wren** (a bird). Product name must not be another bird, must not
  be avian-adjacent (Nest, Perch, Roost, Aviary, Flock...), and must not rhyme
  with "Wren" when said aloud.
- No privacy/crypto signalling (Cipher, Vault, Shield, Lock, Crypt, etc.)
- Short, sayable, non-corporate. A place you'd hang out.
- `.com` being taken is expected for real English words. `.chat` is thematically
  perfect and a realistic primary domain. Coined names are the realistic route
  to a free `.com`.

## Where this landed — an honest verdict

**No candidate in this round is clean.** The finding is structural, not bad luck:
every short, real, pronounceable English word is both domain-registered and
product-namespaced, several times over. `.org` and `.xyz` returned TAKEN for all
seven survivors. That is the market, not a failure of the shortlist.

What survived is a choice between two compromises:

### The best available address
**Revel** — `revel.chat` is genuinely free (verified twice). *Revel* means to
celebrate, to take pleasure in company; a revel is a gathering. The name has the
right warmth and it is a place-word in the way this product wants. Its
collisions are numerous but all sit in restaurant POS, digital signage, EV
rideshare and hardware control — nobody would confuse a consumer chat app for
any of them. **This is the strongest practical pick from this round.**

### The best meanings, worse addresses
- **Stoop** — `stoop.gg` free. A stoop is *literally the place people sit and
  talk* in a neighbourhood, which is the most on-thesis metaphor anything
  generated. Undercut by the verb ("stoop to"), and `.chat` is gone.
- **Sotto** — `sotto.gg` free. From *sotto voce*, "in a low voice" — quiet,
  private speech. Lovely fit. Undercut by a crowded namespace including an
  on-device dictation tool.

### The best domains, weakest names
**Tova** and **Vell** hold both `.chat` and `.gg`, and are the least
distinctive words on the list with the most crowded app namespaces. Good
addresses attached to forgettable names.

## Recommendation

Take **Revel** (`revel.chat`) if you want to stop here — it's defensible and the
address is real.

Otherwise, run **one more round restricted to coined, pronounceable names**.
That is the only strategy with a realistic path to a clean `.com` *and* an empty
product namespace, and this round demonstrates why: real words have no room
left. Round 2 barely explored coinages before it was cut short.

---

## Caveat

Whois and RDAP are strong signals, not proof. Premium/reserved names, registry
holds, and trademarks can all make a "free" domain unbuyable or a "taken" domain
acquirable. Confirm at a registrar before committing money. Collision searches
above are indicative, not a trademark search.

## Round 3 — `.gg` first, meaning-first (2026-08-27)

Two corrections to the earlier rounds, both from Viola:

**1. `.gg` is probably the right TLD, not `.com` or `.chat`.** There's a real
product argument, not just an aesthetic one: Discord's invite links are
`discord.gg/xyz`, so `<name>.gg/<code>` is a pattern the target audience already
reads fluently as "this is a community invite". For a Discord alternative that is
free legibility. `.gg` is also short, memorable, and universally parsed as
"good game" rather than as Guernsey.

**2. A name has to do semantic work.** *Tova* was the round-2 domain winner and
it should not have been in the running: it's a Hebrew/Scandinavian given name
(feminine of *tov*, "good") that says nothing about places, conversation or
gathering. It got in because the generator's "short non-English words" strategy
optimises for **sound and availability**, which is exactly how you end up with a
pleasant noise that carries no meaning. *Vell* has the same defect. Both dropped.

**The rule going forward:** the definition must map onto the product. Haunt,
Hollow, Stoop, Sotto and Mews all pass. Tova and Vell don't.

### Where it stands

**Mews** is the best result of the whole audit. `mews.gg` *and* `mews.chat` are
both free — the only candidate anywhere in three rounds to hold both — and the
definition does real work: a mews is a quiet enclosed lane of converted stables
off a main road, a small residential courtyard. Enclosed, private, a handful of
homes around a shared space. It's one syllable, said "myooz".

Two honest caveats:

- **Mews Systems** is a large, well-funded hospitality PMS company. It's the same
  shape of collision as Revel's (real company, completely different industry, no
  consumer would confuse them) — but it is bigger than any of Revel's.
- **Etymology snag:** "mews" comes from falconry — the mews was where hawks were
  kept while moulting. Given the mascot is **Wren**, that's a faint avian echo.
  Most people know the word as a London street type and will never make the
  connection, but it's there and it should be a conscious choice rather than a
  surprise later.

**The choice is now:** `mews.gg` (both TLDs, better place-metaphor, bigger
company collision, faint bird echo) versus `revel.chat` (one TLD, warmer word,
smaller scattered collisions, no `.gg`).

## Decision (2026-08-27): **Revel** — `revel.chat`

Viola's call. `revel.chat` re-verified free twice via RDAP at decision time.
`revel.gg` is taken, so the `.gg` idea doesn't survive contact with this name —
it was a preference, not a requirement.

**Also considered at the last minute:** `kitten.chat` and `kitten.gg` are both
taken. **`kitties.chat` and `kitties.gg` are both free** — genuinely the best
domain position found in the entire audit. Passed over anyway, and the reasoning
is worth keeping:

- It's an **audience-signalling** name, not a product-describing one. It says who
  we are rather than what this is, which builds a strong in-group identity and
  simultaneously caps the product at that in-group. Hard to walk back later.
- There is a **credibility cost** specific to *this* product. We're asking people
  to trust our cryptography with conversations that matter to them; "kitties.chat"
  spends credibility we need for the recovery-code screen and the threat model.
- "Kitten" carries adult/kink connotations that make it awkward for communities
  that aren't that, and this is meant to be a general-purpose chat app.

None of which makes it a bad name — it makes it a name for a different product,
or for a fork/skin of this one. Worth remembering it's sitting there unregistered.

**Action:** register `revel.chat` sooner rather than later. Availability was
checked via public RDAP/whois; the window between checking a name and owning it
is not a place to linger.

**Still open:** the mark. It's currently three overlapping discs, which was drawn
against no name at all. "Revel" — a gathering, celebration, people together —
suits it fine, but it deserves a deliberate pass now that there's a word.
